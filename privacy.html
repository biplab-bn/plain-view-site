Privacy policy

This one policy covers every app published by plain.view. Last updated: 16 October 2026.

The short version. Notepad asks for no Android permissions at all, and collects nothing. Soft Keys asks for permissions that do specific, visible jobs — locking the screen and opening the power menu — and also collects nothing. VentoyDroid is different: it shows advertising supplied by Google, and it lets you email an error report if you choose to. None of these apps has a user account, none of them has a server of its own, and none of them sends your notes, your files or your ISO images anywhere. This website sets no cookies and runs no analytics.

Who this policy is from

plain.view is the developer name under which these apps are published. It is one independent developer, not a company, and there is no team, no server and no data centre behind it. Every app published under this name is covered by this single policy rather than by a separate one, so that there is one place to read and one place to change.

App stores ask for a privacy policy URL per app. This page is that URL for all of them.

Apps covered by this policy

  • Notepad: Private Notes Android note-taking app. No permissions, no data collected.
  • Soft Keys - Volume & Power Controller Android app for volume, lock and power controls. Uses Device Administrator and an Accessibility service. No data collected.
  • VentoyDroid Android app that installs the Ventoy bootloader onto a USB stick. Shows Google advertising. Collects no files or writing.

No web apps are published yet. When one is, it will be named here and added to the table below before it goes live.

What the apps store

Notepad keeps the notes you write in a database on your own device. Soft Keys keeps its preferences, such as whether haptic feedback is on, as local settings on your device. VentoyDroid keeps its settings and the state of an install in progress on your device, and writes to the USB stick you connect and confirm.

None of this is uploaded anywhere, because none of these apps has a server of its own to upload to.

What is not collected

Across all the apps listed above, except where the VentoyDroid section below says otherwise:

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no email address or name
  • No analytics, and no usage statistics
  • No crash reports or diagnostics sent automatically
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No cookies, no trackers, and no third-party SDK that transmits anything
  • No access to your contacts, photos, location or files beyond the single item you hand to the app yourself
  • Your notes, your text and your ISO files are never read by anyone but you

Permissions

An app can only do what Android permits it to do, so this is the list for each app. These are the permissions declared in the app's own manifest.

Notepad: Private Notes

None. Notepad declares no Android permissions at all — not even internet access. Sharing a note, importing a file and exporting a note all use the standard Android system dialogs, which grant access to the single item you pick rather than to your whole device.

Soft Keys - Volume & Power Controller

This is not a zero-permission app. It requests:

Device Administrator
Used for one thing only: locking the screen, as a replacement for a broken power button. It is not used to erase the device, change passwords or read anything.
Accessibility service
Used only to open the system power menu. The service is deliberately minimal: it is declared with canRetrieveWindowContent="false" and requests no event types, so it cannot read what is on your screen. It opens one menu when you press one button.
Foreground service
Keeps the notification control panel alive so the phone can be controlled from the shade.
Notifications
The control panel itself. On Android 13 and newer this is asked for at the time it is first needed.
Boot completed
Restores the control panel after the phone restarts.
Modify audio settings
Changes the volume streams the app controls.
Vibrate
Optional haptic feedback. No vibration happens unless you turn it on.

Soft Keys does not hold the internet permission. It has no way to send anything anywhere.

VentoyDroid

VentoyDroid requests:

USB host access
Access to the USB stick you plug in, granted by you for that one device. It is what lets the app write a Ventoy bootloader to the stick without needing any storage permission for your phone.
Internet
For the Google advertising described below, and nothing else.
Network state
Tells the advertising library whether a connection is available.
Foreground service
Keeps a long install from being killed part-way by the system, and keeps the screen awake while it runs.
Notifications
The progress of an install, and the buttons to cancel it.
Wake lock
Stops the phone sleeping mid-write, which is the one moment where an interruption could damage the stick.
Advertising ID and ad-services permissions
Added to the app by Google's AdMob library, not written by hand: com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID and the ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS permissions.

VentoyDroid does not request permission to read your phone's storage. It writes only to the stick you select and confirm.

Advertising

Advertising appears in VentoyDroid only. Notepad and Soft Keys contain no advertising code at all.

VentoyDroid shows a banner supplied by Google AdMob, and one full-screen advertisement after an install finishes. Because Google serves those advertisements, Google collects the data its own policies describe, which can include your device identifiers, IP address, which advertisements you saw and interacted with, and approximate location. That collection is controlled by Google, not by plain.view, and is governed by the Google Privacy Policy. plain.view never receives it.

Where the law requires it — in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland — a Google consent form appears before any advertisement is requested. Your answer is stored on your device by Google's consent library. If you decline, or simply do not answer, advertisements do not load and the rest of the app works exactly as it always does.

If you want to use VentoyDroid without any part of it touching the network, the honest answer is that this is not currently possible: the advertising is part of that app's build.

Data you choose to share

Sharing is always something you start, never something that happens in the background.

  • If you use Notepad's Share button, or share text into Notepad, that text is passed between the two apps by Android. Once a note is saved it stays on your device. Exporting a note writes a file to the location you pick, and that file is then yours.
  • Soft Keys shares nothing. You press its buttons; it changes your phone's volume, locks the screen or opens the power menu.
  • If you tap Report this problem in VentoyDroid, your own mail app opens with a report already written. Nothing is sent until you press send in that mail app, and you can read and edit the whole message first, or simply not send it. A report contains only technical details: app version and build type, device manufacturer and model, Android version and API level, a shortened build fingerprint, supported processor types, device language, free internal storage, whether battery optimisation is disabled, the error message and stack trace, and recent app log lines.
  • The USB stick you give VentoyDroid is written to because you selected and confirmed it. Everything already on that stick is erased, which the app asks you to confirm before it starts.

Backups

If you have Android's system backup switched on, app data is included in your device backup, which Google encrypts and stores in your Google account. For Notepad that means your notes and settings; for the other apps it means their settings and local state. This is a feature of Android, not of these apps, and you can turn it off in your device settings.

Data retention and deletion

  • Deleting a note in Notepad moves it to the trash. Trashed notes are removed automatically after 30 days, "Empty trash" removes them immediately, and uninstalling the app removes its data.
  • Uninstalling any of these apps removes the data it kept on your device.
  • An error report you email is kept only as long as it takes to diagnose the fault you reported, and is not used for anything else. Write to the address in the Contact section and any report you sent will be deleted.
  • There is nothing else to delete, because nothing else is held.

Payments

Paid features, where offered, are bought through your device's app store — Google Play or Uptodown — not through the app itself. The store handles your payment details; plain.view never receives or sees your card number or payment credentials. If you buy anything, the store keeps its own record of the purchase and may pass on limited details, such as whether a feature is currently active on your device.

Children

Notepad and Soft Keys collect no data from anyone, so they collect none from children either, and they are suitable for all ages. VentoyDroid is a utility for installing bootable USB sticks and is not directed at children; the advertising library it uses applies Google's own age and consent safeguards.

This website

The site you are reading sets no cookies, runs no analytics, has no comment form and loads nothing from another domain. It is plain HTML, CSS and a small script, so there is nothing here to collect and nothing to ask you to consent to. That is why you have not been shown a cookie banner.
